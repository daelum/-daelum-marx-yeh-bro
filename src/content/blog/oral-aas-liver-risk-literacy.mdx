**Oral AAS** (anabolic-androgenic steroids taken by mouth) show up a lot in gym harm-reduction talk because of how some compounds are engineered to survive digestion — and because forums treat liver enzymes like a video-game HUD. This page stays **class-level**: why liver stress enters the conversation, what stacking with alcohol does in plain language, and why labs belong with a clinician. It is **not** a list of what to take, how much, how long, or where to buy. Yeh Bro does not sell or source AAS, SARMs, or grey gear.

## TL;DR

- Some oral AAS patterns are discussed in clinical and public-health literature alongside **hepatic (liver) stress** signals — that does not make Discord a hepatology clinic.
- “I feel fine” ≠ enzyme trends — [PED bloodwork literacy](/blog/ped-bloodwork-literacy) · [bloodwork for lifters](/blog/bloodwork-for-lifters).
- Alcohol on top is a quiet way to make a bad idea worse — [alcohol and training](/blog/alcohol-training-quiet-tax).

- Prefer not escalating; know legal risk where you live; medical honesty if someone proceeds despite that.

## Why “orals” get special mention (without a cookbook)

In gym slang, “orals” often means AAS tablets/capsules as opposed to injected androgens. Harm-reduction education flags them because:

1. **First-pass metabolism** — the liver sees a lot of what you swallow.  
2. **Historical patterns** of enzyme elevations in literature and clinic anecdotes (still individual).  
3. **Forum culture** that treats supplements as permission slips.

We will **not** name dose tables, “least toxic oral” rankings, or duration recipes. That slides into how-to. Class language only — see compliance posture in the [PED hub](/blog/ped-harm-reduction-australia).

## Legal reality (high-level, not advice)

In many countries — including Australia — anabolic steroids are tightly controlled. Possession, supply, and importation can carry serious consequences. Online branding does not legalise a substance. Verify with official sources (e.g. TGA / Border Force context) and independent counsel if you need advice.

## What people should take to a clinician (education)

If someone is already using despite legal and health warnings, concealing it blocks care. Topics clinicians may care about conceptually:

| Theme | Why it comes up |
|---|---|
| Liver enzymes (ALT, AST, etc.) | Pattern + history — not one scary screenshot |
| Symptoms | Jaundice, severe abdominal pain, dark urine, unusual fatigue — urgent care territory |
| Meds / alcohol / viral hepatitis history | Stacking risks |
| Plan to stop | [Coming off under care](/blog/ped-coming-off-under-care) |

Ordering and interpreting labs is medical care for *you* — not a Yeh Bro checklist.
