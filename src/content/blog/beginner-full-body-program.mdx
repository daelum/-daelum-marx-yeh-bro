**Scope:** Beginner training · no fancy split theatre.

If you’re new (or coming back after a long break), start with [Start here](/start-here). This is the training piece of that spine — not a 47-exercise “bro split” you abandon by week three.

## TL;DR

- Lift **full body 3× per week** (e.g. Mon / Wed / Fri).
- Hit **squat or hinge, push, pull, and a carry/core** each session.
- Add a little load or a couple of reps over time — that’s [progressive overload](/blog/progressive-overload).
- Leave **1–3 reps in the tank** most sets. Ego failure is not a plan.
- Walk most days. Sleep like it’s part of the program — [sleep for lifters](/blog/sleep-for-lifters).

## Why full body first

Most beginners don’t need six different “chest days.” They need:

1. Practice on the big lifts often enough to get skilled
2. Enough recovery between sessions
3. A schedule that survives a normal week

Full body three times a week hits each pattern ~3×. You get practice without living in the gym. When life is messy, three sessions is still a complete week — see [how many days you should lift](/blog/how-many-days-to-lift).

## The template (A / B optional)

Alternate **A** and **B** across the week so you don’t grind the exact same angles every time. Same idea either day: big patterns, controlled effort.

### Day A

| Pattern | Example | Sets × reps |
|---|---|---|
| Squat | Goblet squat or back squat | 3 × 6–10 |
| Horizontal push | Dumbbell or barbell bench | 3 × 6–10 |
| Horizontal pull | Chest-supported row or cable row | 3 × 8–12 |
| Hinge assist | Romanian deadlift | 2–3 × 8–12 |
| Carry / core | Farmer carry or plank | 2–3 rounds |

### Day B

| Pattern | Example | Sets × reps |
|---|---|---|
| Hinge | Trap-bar or conventional deadlift (conservative) | 3 × 5–8 |
| Vertical push | Overhead press | 3 × 6–10 |
| Vertical pull | Lat pulldown or assisted pull-up | 3 × 6–10 |
| Squat assist | Leg press or split squat | 2–3 × 8–12 |
| Carry / core | Suitcase carry or dead bug | 2–3 rounds |

Swap machines for free weights if that’s what you have. The **pattern** matters more than the brand of rack.

## Effort and progression

- Warm up with empty bar / light sets until the movement feels smooth.
- Work sets: stop with **1–3 reps left**. If form breaks, you went too far.
- When you hit the top of the rep range on all sets with good form, **add a small amount of load** next time. Details: [Progressive overload](/blog/progressive-overload).
- If joints feel wrecked or performance tanks for a week, run a [deload](/blog/deload-week) — don’t just add caffeine.

## Cardio without wrecking recovery

- **Walk most days** — easy, sustainable NEAT. Full guide: [Walking for lifters](/blog/walking-for-lifters).
- Optional: one harder conditioning session if sleep and lifts are holding.
- Daily redline HIIT on top of hard lifting is how beginners burn out.

## Food and recovery (don’t skip)

Training only works if the other levers aren’t a dumpster fire:

- Protein in a practical range — [Protein targets without obsession](/blog/protein-targets-for-lifters)
- Sleep window you can protect — [Sleep for lifters](/blog/sleep-for-lifters)
- Creatine if you want the easy win — [How to dose creatine](/blog/how-to-dose-creatine) · [/go/creatine-true](/go/creatine-true)

## When to change the split

Stay on full body until:

- You’re hitting **3 sessions most weeks** for 8–12+ weeks
- The big lifts feel skilled, not scary
- You’re hungry for more volume on specific patterns

Then Upper / Lower 4× is a fair next step — still covered in [how many training days](/blog/how-many-days-to-lift). Program-hopping every Monday is procrastination dressed as optimisation.
