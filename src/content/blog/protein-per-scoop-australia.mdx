## TL;DR

- **Protein per scoop (g)** on the nutrition panel beats marketing “30 g!” callouts.
- Scoop size (g of powder) ≠ protein grams.
- Rank tubs with **AUD per 25 g protein** after shipping.
- Red flags: serving games, proprietary protein blends, panel/claim mismatch, hormone cosplay.

## Scoop vs protein (the mix-up)

| Number on the tub | What it means |
|---|---|
| Scoop weight (e.g. 40 g) | How much **powder** is in a serve |
| Protein per serve (e.g. 25 g) | How much **protein** that serve delivers |
| “% protein” marketing | Often powder composition talk — still verify the panel |

A 40 g scoop with 22 g protein is normal for some concentrates. A “tiny scoop” with flashy % can still be a bad AUD deal.

## Do this before you buy

```
protein_per_serve = panel value (g)
total_protein ≈ protein_per_serve × serves
AUD_per_25g = (price_AUD_incl_shipping ÷ total_protein) × 25
```

## Label red flags AU buyers miss

| Flag | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Front claim ≠ panel | You’re buying the panel |
| Proprietary “protein blend” with no source order clarity | Hard to audit |
| Tiny scoop + huge serve count | Economics look fake-cheap |
| Mass gainer sold as “protein” | You’re buying carbs/calories |
| US hype + quiet AU freight | Landed AUD / 25 g collapses |
| Hormone / PCT / AAS-cosplay branding | Hard skip — not phase-1 Yeh Bro |

## WPC vs WPI (quick)

- **Concentrate:** usually more powder per gram of protein; often best AUD / 25 g.
- **Isolate:** higher protein per scoop, less lactose — pay for tolerance/macros.

## FAQ

**Is 25 g per scoop the law?**  
No. It’s a common serve target. Judge daily protein total, not one magic scoop.

**Does a bigger scoop mean better protein?**  
No — bigger scoop can mean more lactose, fat, flavouring. Read protein grams.

**Can supermarket own-brand win?**  
Yes if panel + AUD / 25 g clear the quality floor.

**Women’s “special” scoops?**  
Same maths. Skip pink tax unless the panel/price wins.
