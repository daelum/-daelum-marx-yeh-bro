You ate sushi and woke up “up two kilos.” That isn’t two kilos of body fat overnight. **Sodium**, carbohydrate, creatine, and hydration shift water. Panic-cutting after one weigh-in is how adherence dies — and how people invent salt rituals that fix nothing useful.

## TL;DR

- Daily scale noise is mostly **water and glycogen**, not fat.
- Trend **weekly averages**; keep waist and photos in the loop — [tracking](/blog/tracking-weight-waist-photos).
- Creatine can bump weight — keep taking it — [bloat myth](/blog/does-creatine-cause-bloating) · [Muscle Nation creatine monohydrate](/go/creatine-amz).
- Don’t crash water or run DIY “deplete” protocols from contest forums — [PED hub](/blog/ped-harm-reduction-australia) literacy only; no stage-cut cosplay for a Tuesday weigh-in.
- Fix the week’s calories and protein if the *trend* is wrong — not last night’s soy sauce.

## Common swing sources

| Trigger | What often happens |
|---|---|
| High-sodium restaurant meal | Temporary water retention |
| Carb refeed | Glycogen + water — [refeeds](/blog/refeeds-and-diet-breaks) |
| Starting creatine | Scale bump ≠ fat |
| Hard training + inflammation | Short-term weight blip |
| Alcohol night | Sleep + water chaos — [alcohol tax](/blog/alcohol-training-quiet-tax) |
| Low sleep | Hunger + scale weirdness — [sleep](/blog/sleep-for-lifters) |
| Travel / heat / long flights | Hydration and sodium swings |
| Menstrual cycle (when relevant) | Water weight patterns — track weeks, not one morning |

**Practical fact vs panic story:** body fat doesn’t appear or vanish in meaningful amounts overnight. Water and glycogen do move that fast. Treat a single morning number as weather, not climate.

## What the scale is (and isn’t) telling you

The scale measures everything you’re carrying that morning — food still in the gut, water, muscle, fat, glycogen. It’s useful as a **trend tool**, useless as a moral grade. Pair it with:

- Weekly waist (same tape, same spot)
- Monthly boring photos — [photo lighting honesty](/blog/photo-lighting-honesty-for-lifters)
- Logbook strength — [progressive overload](/blog/progressive-overload)

If the waist is down and lifts are stable while the scale wiggles, you’re probably fine.

## What to do instead of panic

1. Weigh the next 5–7 mornings (similar conditions); average them.
2. Check waist weekly, not hourly photos under new lighting.
3. Keep protein and steps steady — [meal structure](/blog/meal-structure-without-apps) · [steps](/blog/step-targets-without-obsessing-neat).
4. If the *weekly* trend is wrong for your goal, adjust calories calmly — [recalculating calories](/blog/recalculating-calories-after-weight-change) — not salt rituals.
5. Stay on creatine if you use it; don’t stop for a “dry” photo that means nothing next week.

Intra electrolytes on sweaty days are about performance and comfort, not a cut hack — [intra electrolytes](/blog/intra-workout-electrolytes-australia) · [intra carbs](/blog/intra-carbs-for-long-sessions).

## Contests, forums, and bad ideas

Stage-peak water and sodium games belong to prepared athletes under coaching — not to someone mid-cut who saw a spike after pizza. Copying forum “deplete” lore into a normal week is a health and adherence risk. For culture literacy only: [contest-prep culture vs health](/blog/contest-prep-culture-vs-health) · [PED harm reduction](/blog/ped-harm-reduction-australia). This site does not teach DIY dehydration protocols.

## Common mistakes

- Slashing 500 calories after one salty dinner
- Dropping creatine because the scale ticked up
- Weighing six times a day and rewriting the plan each time
- Comparing today’s number to a dehydrated, pumped, golden-hour photo week
- Letting scale panic feed body-image spirals — [body dysmorphia adjacency](/blog/body-dysmorphia-adjacency-gym-culture)

## Takeaways

Salt isn’t your enemy; impatience is. Read weeks, not mornings. Keep training and protein steady; change calories only when the trend says so — [Yeh Bro System](/start-here).
