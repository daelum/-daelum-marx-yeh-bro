**Scope:** Training effort literacy · pairs with [progressive overload](/blog/progressive-overload) · [Yeh Bro System · training](/start-here#step-2).

“Train hard” is useless advice if hard means grinding form into the floor every set. **RPE** (rate of perceived exertion) and **RIR** (reps in reserve) are just tools to say: *how close to failure was that set?* Use them so you can progress for months instead of frying yourself by Thursday.

## TL;DR

- **RIR** = how many clean reps you could still do. **RPE** ≈ 10 minus RIR on many lifting scales (RPE 7 ≈ 3 RIR).
- Most hypertrophy / strength work for non-competitors: finish sets around **1–3 RIR** (roughly RPE 7–9).
- True failure every set is a seasoning, not the meal — especially on squats and hinges.
- If the logbook won’t move, fix sleep, food, stress, or [deload](/blog/deload-week) before inventing a new “intensity technique.”

## Plain definitions

| Term | Meaning in the gym |
|---|---|
| **Reps in reserve (RIR)** | Clean reps left before form breaks or you miss |
| **Rate of perceived exertion (RPE)** | How hard the set felt on a 1–10 scale (lifting charts often map to RIR) |
| **Technical failure** | Form collapses even if the ego wants one more |
| **Absolute failure** | Cannot complete the rep |

You don’t need a PhD spreadsheet. You need honesty: *could I have done two more with the same form?*

## A practical RPE ↔ RIR cheat sheet

| Feel after the set | Approx RIR | Approx RPE |
|---|---|---|
| Could do 4+ easy | 4+ | ≤6 |
| Could do ~3 more clean | 3 | ~7 |
| Could do ~2 more | 2 | ~8 |
| Maybe 1 ugly grinder | 1 | ~9 |
| Nothing left / form gone | 0 | 10 |

Beginners often **under**-rate effort (they sandbag) or **over**-rate it (everything feels like death). Both improve with a logbook and the same lifts for weeks — [progressive overload](/blog/progressive-overload).

## Where to live most of the time

For the templates in [full body](/blog/beginner-full-body-program) and [upper / lower](/blog/upper-lower-split):

- **Main compounds:** ~**2–3 RIR** most weeks
- **Accessories:** ~**1–3 RIR** — slightly closer is fine when the movement is safer
- **Top sets near a meet / test day:** optional closer proximity — not every Tuesday

Leaving reps in the tank is not “going easy.” It’s how you recover enough to add load next week.

## Ego failure vs useful hard sets

| Ego failure | Useful hard set |
|---|---|
| Spine rounds on the last three reps | Form holds; bar speed slows |
| Spotter bench-presses half the set | You owned the range of motion |
| You’re wrecked for three days after “chest day” | You’re sore / tired but sessions stay on schedule |

If every session needs a pre-workout personality transplant, check [caffeine dose](/blog/caffeine-dose-training) and [sleep](/blog/sleep-for-lifters) before blaming the program.

## How RIR pairs with progressive overload

1. Pick a rep range (e.g. 6–10).
2. Work at ~2 RIR.
3. When all work sets hit the top of the range with ~2 RIR left, **add load**.
4. If you miss reps or form breaks early, you guessed RIR wrong — or recovery is trash.

That’s the whole game. Fancy periodisation can wait.

## Common failure modes

- **Sandbagging forever** — “3 RIR” that was actually 6. Film a set occasionally.
- **Daily RPE 10 cosplay** — especially on deadlifts. Your back will send the invoice.
- **Cutting hard + max effort** — deficit already taxes recovery — [cutting guide](/blog/cutting-without-wrecking-lifts).
- **Coach who only sells failure** — red flag territory — [online coach red flags](/blog/online-coach-red-flags).
