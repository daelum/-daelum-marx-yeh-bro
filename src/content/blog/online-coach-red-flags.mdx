There’s real coaching out there: honest assessments, boring progressive plans, food that fits your life. There’s also a content economy that sells **certainty, speed, and someone else’s genetics** with a checkout link. This piece is how to tell the difference without quitting the gym in protest.

## TL;DR

- Good coaching makes your **week repeatable**. Bad coaching makes your **feed jealous**.
- Red flags: impossible timelines, one PDF for every human, shame-based check-ins, quiet PED pressure, and programs that exist to sell tubs.
- You can run [Yeh Bro System](/start-here) without a guru — full body, protein, sleep, creatine — and hire help later if you want.
- Literacy ≠ conspiracy. Some physiques include undisclosed variables — [natty or not](/blog/natty-or-not-physique-standards).

## What good coaching usually looks like

| Signal | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Asks about training age, sleep, injuries, schedule | Programming needs constraints |
| Starts simpler than your ego wants | Skill and consistency beat novelty |
| Reviews the logbook | Progressive overload is the product — [overload guide](/blog/progressive-overload) |
| Adjusts when life spikes | Stress is a training variable — [stress and lifting](/blog/stress-and-lifting) |
| Clear scope (they’re not your doctor) | Medical claims stay with clinicians |

Boring is a feature.

## Red flags (the greatest hits)

### 1. Eight-week miracle physiques as the sales engine

Lighting, pumps, filters, long training ages, and sometimes pharmacology sit behind a lot of “client results.” If the pitch is “look like this by summer” with no talk of maintenance, you’re buying a trailer, not a process.

### 2. One macro sheet to rule them all

Everyone gets 200 g protein and a grocery list from 2014. No discussion of preferences, budget, or [carbs around training](/blog/carbs-around-training). Cookie-cutter can work *briefly*; it’s not coaching.

### 3. Failure cosplay as personality

Every set to absolute failure, every day. No [RPE / reps in reserve](/blog/rpe-reps-in-reserve), no [deload](/blog/deload-week) language. Injury and burnout are not proof of seriousness.

### 4. Shame check-ins

Missed a session → moral lecture. Real adults miss sessions. Adults reschedule — [how many days](/blog/how-many-days-to-lift).

### 5. Quiet (or loud) PED pressure

“You’ll never look like X natural” as a sales funnel toward grey-market advice. Culture literacy is fine; **pressure to escalate** is not coaching. We don’t source gear — [PED harm reduction](/blog/ped-harm-reduction-australia).

### 6. Supplement cart before movement pattern

The “program” is a stack screenshot. Order of operations: train, food, sleep, *then* optional powders — [beginner stack](/blog/beginner-supplement-stack-australia) · [label red flags](/blog/supplement-label-red-flags-australia).

### 7. Medical theatre without credentials

Hormone protocols, “PCT guides,” peptide menus from a coach with no prescribing rights. That’s not edgy — that’s dangerous cosplay. Bloodwork and meds stay with a clinician — [bloodwork for lifters](/blog/bloodwork-for-lifters).

## Affiliate honesty (including ours)

Coaches and sites (including Yeh Bro) may earn from **legal** sports-nutrition links. The test: does the content still help if you buy nothing today? Soft product picks belong inside useful guides — see [disclosure](/disclosure) — not as the spine of the advice.

Live convenience examples we actually hop (education context, not a coach upsell): [Muscle Nation creatine monohydrate](/go/creatine-amz) · [Muscle Nation whey isolate](/go/protein-amz).

## DIY bar before you hire anyone

Run twelve weeks of:

1. [Full body](/blog/beginner-full-body-program) or [upper / lower](/blog/upper-lower-split)
2. [Protein](/blog/protein-targets-for-lifters) most days
3. [Sleep](/blog/sleep-for-lifters) window protected
4. Logbook progressing — [progressive overload](/blog/progressive-overload)

If that’s solid and you want eyes on technique or peaking, hire carefully. If you can’t do the boring week, a Discord coach won’t magically install discipline.

Scepticism protects your money and your health. Cynicism that stops you training is just another trap. Keep the process; bin the circus — [Yeh Bro System](/start-here).
