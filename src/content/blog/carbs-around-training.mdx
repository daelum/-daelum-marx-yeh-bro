Protein gets the gym-bro billboard. **Carbohydrates** still do the boring job of fuelling hard sessions and filling muscle glycogen so you don’t feel like a wet noodle on squat day. You don’t need a 47-step “carb cycling” spreadsheet on day one — you need enough carbs in the day, with a bias near training when it helps.

## TL;DR

- Hit [protein](/blog/protein-targets-for-lifters) first; then place enough **carbs** so training quality holds.
- Bias some carbs **before / around** the session if you feel flat; total daily intake still matters more than minute-perfect timing.
- In a **cut**, protect training carbs before you protect late-night cereal theatre — [cutting guide](/blog/cutting-without-wrecking-lifts).
- In a **lean bulk**, carbs help the surplus feel trainable — [lean bulk basics](/blog/lean-bulk-basics).
- Intra carbs / fancy gels are optional for most gym sessions under ~90 minutes — [intra electrolytes](/blog/intra-workout-electrolytes-australia).

## What carbs are doing for lifters

| Job | Gym translation |
|---|---|
| Glycogen | Fuel for higher-rep and glycolytic work |
| Training quality | Better bar speed and “pop” when you’re not under-fuelled |
| Recovery context | Helps refill stores between sessions (with food + sleep) |
| Hormonal / mood noise | Very low-carb + hard lifting + life stress is a common crash cocktail |

Carbs aren’t “dirty.” Excess calories from any macro can add fat. Under-fuelling is how people blame the program when they just showed up empty.

## Timing that actually helps most people

### Before training (60–180 minutes)

A meal or snack with **carbs + some protein**: rice, potato, oats, fruit, bread, yoghurt + granola — whatever you digest well. Goal: show up with energy, not a food baby mid-set.

If you train fasted and feel fine, cool — but if performance tanks, stop cosplaying intermittent fasting as a personality and eat.

### During (optional)

Most 45–75 minute gym sessions: **water + maybe electrolytes** if you sweat hard. Intra carbs matter more for long endurance or extremely high-volume days — see [intra electrolytes](/blog/intra-workout-electrolytes-australia).

### After

Protein + carbs in the hours after training is practical, not magic. The “anabolic window” isn’t a 20-minute panic. Get the day’s totals right; use the post-session meal as an easy place to land them — [whey vs food](/blog/whey-vs-food-protein).

## Cut vs bulk: where carbs get cut first (and shouldn’t)

| Phase | Carb approach |
|---|---|
| **Deficit** | Keep enough around training to protect lifts; trim leisure snacks and liquid calories first |
| **Maintenance** | Eat enough that sessions feel repeatable |
| **Lean surplus** | Add carbs (and some fat) slowly so scale weight creeps without dirty-bulk chaos |

Using zero-cal stims to paper over a wrecked cut: [energy drinks vs pre in a deficit](/blog/energy-drinks-vs-pre-workout-deficit). Stims aren’t carbohydrates.

## Simple decision table

| You feel… | First move |
|---|---|
| Flat mid-session, sleep is fine | Add carbs in the pre-training meal |
| Strong in the gym, scale drifting up unwanted | Check total calories — not “ban rice” |
| Wired but empty | Food first; caffeine second — [caffeine dose](/blog/caffeine-dose-training) |
| Hungry between meals on a cut | Protein + volume vegetables; optional near-zero ritual drink — not another pastry |

## Powder isn’t a carb strategy

Protein powder fills **protein** gaps. Creatine fills a **strength support** gap. Neither replaces rice.
