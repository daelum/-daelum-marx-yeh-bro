Flavour fights are entertainment. **Scoop maths** decides who wins. For label red flags and “protein per scoop vs powder weight,” see [protein per scoop](/blog/protein-per-scoop-australia) — this page is the **price maths**.

## TL;DR

- Divide tub price by total protein grams in the tub, then normalise to **$ per 25 g protein**. That’s the fair fight.
- Big **whey protein concentrate (WPC)** bags on sale often win on cost. **Whey protein isolate (WPI)** earns a premium when lactose or leaner macros matter — not for ego.
- Don’t invent live prices here; run the formula on the label the day you buy.
- Powder is convenience. Food still carries most of the week — [whey vs food](/blog/whey-vs-food-protein).

## The formula

```
$ / 25 g protein = (tub price AUD ÷ total protein grams in tub) × 25
```

Or: price ÷ serves ÷ protein-per-serve × 25.

**Example framing (illustrative numbers only):** a $60 tub with 30 serves × 25 g protein = 750 g total protein → $60 ÷ 750 × 25 ≈ **$2.00 per 25 g**. Another tub at $80 with 28 serves × 27 g = 756 g → ≈ **$2.65 per 25 g**. The louder brand just lost on maths.

Do it on the label in front of you. Promotions change weekly. Shipping, subscription discounts, and “bonus scoop” marketing don’t change the formula — they change the inputs.

## What to read on the label

| Field | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Protein per serve (as sold) | Skip fantasy “dry basis” games when you can |
| Serve size in grams | A 40 g scoop with 22 g protein is not a 30 g scoop with 25 g |
| Serves per container | Needed for total protein grams |
| Added sugars / fillers | Matters more on a cut than on a bulk |
| Form (WPC / WPI / blend / plant) | Drives price *and* gut tolerance |

**Lactose wrecks you?** Isolate can be worth the premium. That’s a gut call, not a detox — full comparison: [Whey concentrate vs isolate](/blog/whey-concentrate-vs-isolate-australia). Plant options: [best plant protein](/blog/best-plant-protein-australia) — still run $ / 25 g; don’t assume “plant” means expensive or cheap until you calculate.

## Common mistakes

- Comparing **tub price** instead of protein grams (a $45 tub can lose to a $70 bag)
- Ignoring serve size — “one scoop” is not a unit of protein
- Paying isolate prices when concentrate sits fine in your gut
- Treating powder as a performance-enhancing drug (PED) substitute
- Forgetting food protein — powder fills gaps; it doesn’t erase dinner

## Takeaways

Run **$ per 25 g protein** once per purchase. Prefer clear labels and honest serve sizes. Upgrade to isolate when lactose or macros justify it. Put leftover budget into creatine and training consistency — not the loudest flavour drop.
