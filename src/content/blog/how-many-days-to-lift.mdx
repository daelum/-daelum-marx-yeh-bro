**Scope:** Training frequency · [Start here](/start-here) step 2.

“How many days?” is usually the wrong first question. The right one is: **how many sessions can you repeat for the next 12 weeks?** Then pick a split that fits that number.

## TL;DR

- **Default for most:** **3–4 lifting days** per week.
- **2 days** still builds if those sessions are serious and consistent.
- **5–6 days** can work for experienced lifters with recovery locked — beginners often use extra days to avoid progressing the hard lifts.
- Walk daily. Don’t confuse steps with “another leg day.”

## Match days to life, then to goals

| Days / week | Best fit | Simple structure |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Busy seasons, restart | Full body ×2 |
| 3 | Most beginners / returners | [Full body ×3](/blog/beginner-full-body-program) |
| 4 | Solid intermediate habit | Upper / Lower ×2 |
| 5–6 | High training age + recovery | Push / pull / legs or similar — only if sleep holds |

If you can’t protect sleep and food, extra gym days won’t save you — [sleep](/blog/sleep-for-lifters) · [stress](/blog/stress-and-lifting).

## Why 3–4 is the sweet spot

- Enough practice on big patterns for [progressive overload](/blog/progressive-overload)
- Enough rest that joints and motivation survive
- Miss one day and the week isn’t a write-off

Four days Upper / Lower is excellent once full-body skill is there. You’re not “more hardcore” for splitting chest into two themed holidays.

## Two days still counts

Full body twice a week beats a perfect six-day plan you ghost. Keep sessions complete: squat/hinge, push, pull, carry/core. Progress the logbook. When the calendar opens up, add a third day — don’t reinvent everything.

## When more days backfire

More frequency helps when:

- You’re recovered
- Volume is distributed intelligently
- The main lifts still progress

More frequency fails when:

- Every day is a redline “destroy yourself” session
- You’re cutting hard and sleeping badly — [cutting without wrecking lifts](/blog/cutting-without-wrecking-lifts)
- You’re collecting pump work instead of progressing compounds

If performance or mood tanks, [deload](/blog/deload-week) before adding day six.

## Cardio and “active recovery”

- **Walking** most days supports fat loss and recovery without competing hard with lifting — [walking for lifters](/blog/walking-for-lifters).
- One conditioning session is optional, not mandatory.
- “Rest day” can still include a walk. Rest from *loading*, not from moving.

## A decision tree that doesn’t overthink

1. Can you reliably hit **3** days? → Full body.
2. Hitting 3 easily for months and want more volume? → Upper / Lower **4**.
3. Life exploded? → Drop to **2** full body without shame.
4. Feeling fried? → Deload week, then return to the schedule that was working.
