**Scope:** Training literacy · [Start here · training](/start-here#step-2) · pairs with [progressive overload](/blog/progressive-overload) · [RPE / reps in reserve](/blog/rpe-reps-in-reserve).

If it’s not in the log, it’s a vibe. A training logbook isn’t productivity theatre — it’s how you notice whether last month’s “hard work” actually moved anything. Phone notes app is fine. Fancy spreadsheet optional. Honesty required.

## TL;DR

- Track **date, lift, load, sets × reps, rough RIR (reps in reserve), and one line of notes**.
- Compare **like to like** — same lift, similar sleep/food week — before declaring a plateau.
- Use the log to drive [progressive overload](/blog/progressive-overload), not to shame yourself into ego loads — [ego lifting vs training age](/blog/ego-lifting-vs-training-age).
- Three weeks of blank pages beats a colour-coded template you abandon.

## Minimum viable log

| Field | Example | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Date | 2026-11-01 | Trends need timestamps |
| Session | Upper A | Ties to [upper / lower](/blog/upper-lower-split) or [full body](/blog/beginner-full-body-program) |
| Lift | Bench press | Same name = comparable |
| Work sets | 80 kg × 8, 8, 7 | The actual stimulus |
| RIR / RPE | ~2 RIR | Effort context — [RPE guide](/blog/rpe-reps-in-reserve) |
| Notes | Sleep 5h / left shoulder grumpy | Explains “bad” days |

Optional: body weight weekly average, steps, mood. Don’t track seventeen KPIs on week one.

## How to *read* it (the skill)

### 1. Look for creep, not fireworks

Did the same lift add a rep or a small plate over 2–6 weeks with similar RIR? That’s the game. One magical session after a stim binge is noise — [caffeine dose](/blog/caffeine-dose-training).

### 2. Separate bad weeks from bad programs

Cluster of trash sessions + notes saying “flu / deadline / 4 hours sleep” → fix recovery — [sleep](/blog/sleep-for-lifters) · [stress](/blog/stress-and-lifting) · [rest days](/blog/rest-days-vs-active-recovery).  
Same pattern with good sleep and flat numbers for a long stretch → [plateau checklist](/blog/plateau-checklist-for-lifters) or a [deload](/blog/deload-week).

### 3. Watch form notes more than PR stickers

“Depth disappeared at 100 kg” means you don’t have a 100 kg squat yet. Ego will argue. The log won’t.

### 4. Count sessions completed

Missed two of four every week? You don’t need a new split — you need a schedule you keep — [how many days](/blog/how-many-days-to-lift).

## What not to do

| Trap | Better move |
|---|---|
| New program every Monday | Stay long enough to read a trend |
| Logging only “PRs” | Log normal days — that’s the data |
| Comparing your log to a stranger’s story | Different training age / variables — [natty or not](/blog/natty-or-not-physique-standards) |
| Ignoring food notes on a cut | Flat lifts in a hard deficit are common — [cutting](/blog/cutting-without-wrecking-lifts) · [carbs around training](/blog/carbs-around-training) |

## Tiny template you can paste

```
Date:
Session:
Squat/hinge:
Push:
Pull:
Accessories:
RIR notes:
Sleep / stress / bodyweight:
Next time try:
```

Warmups don’t need a novel — [warmup before lifting](/blog/warmup-before-lifting) — but note if you skipped ramps and paid for it.

## Soft close

The logbook is the coach that doesn’t sell you a stack. Write the boring sets. Read them weekly. Path: [Start here](/start-here).
