A **reverse diet** is a deliberate climb from a deficit toward maintenance (and sometimes into a small surplus) so hunger, training, and hormones-of-stress stop living in the red. It is not magic metabolic witchcraft. It is not a license to erase three months of patience in one weekend.

## TL;DR

- After a hard cut, jump straight to dirty bulk → often fast fat regain + confusion.  
- Reverse: **small calorie bumps** (often via carbs) while watching weekly averages — [tracking weight / waist / photos](/blog/tracking-weight-waist-photos).  
- Keep protein high; keep lifting — [progressive overload](/blog/progressive-overload).  
- Different from a one-day [refeed](/blog/refeeds-and-diet-breaks) or a planned [diet break](/blog/refeeds-and-diet-breaks).

## Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Reverse diet** | Gradual intake increase after a deficit |
| **Diet break** | Hold near maintenance mid-cut |
| **Refeed** | Short higher day inside a cut |
| **Lean bulk** | Small surplus aimed at growth — [lean bulk](/blog/lean-bulk-basics) |
| **Dirty bulk** | Uncontrolled surplus |

## When a reverse earns the slot

- Cut done (you hit a waist/photo goal or adherence is cooked)  
- Lifts flat, cold, hungry, sleep trash  
- You want to grow next without ballooning overnight  

If you’re still happily losing on a moderate deficit with good training, you don’t need a reverse yet.

## A simple approach (not a prescription)

1. Estimate current average intake.  
2. Bump **~50–150 kcal/day** (often carbs) once or twice a week — [carbs around training](/blog/carbs-around-training).  
3. Hold protein — [protein targets](/blog/protein-targets-for-lifters) · [meal structure](/blog/meal-structure-without-apps).  
4. Watch 7-day scale average + waist + gym performance.  
5. When weight stabilises near maintenance and sessions feel alive, either hold or tip into lean surplus.

Hunger noise while reversing is normal — don’t panic-eat the pantry every night — [hunger on a cut](/blog/hunger-on-a-cut) skills still help.

- Keep the program; let RIR improve as fuel returns — [RPE](/blog/rpe-reps-in-reserve).
