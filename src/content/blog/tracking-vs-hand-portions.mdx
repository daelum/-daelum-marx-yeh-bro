**Scope:** Food literacy · hub [calories & macros](/blog/calories-and-macros-for-lifters) · [meal structure without apps](/blog/meal-structure-without-apps) · [Yeh Bro System · food](/start-here#step-3).

You have three tools: a **food scale**, an **app**, and **hand portions**. None is morality. Pick the mode that matches the phase and your brain. Lifters get stuck treating barcode logging as a personality — or treating “intuitive” as code for guessing oil by the tablespoon forever.

## TL;DR

- **Scale + short audit** when you’re guessing wild or stalling — [tracking mistakes](/blog/calorie-tracking-mistakes).
- **Hand portions** for long-term adherence — [meal structure](/blog/meal-structure-without-apps).
- **Apps** as teachers, not lifelong ankle monitors.
- Restaurants need a different playbook — [hitting macros at restaurants](/blog/hitting-macros-at-restaurants).
- Re-weigh when the trend dies; don’t live in decimal-place hell by default.

## Mode comparison

| Mode | Best for | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|
| Food scale | Learning true portions; tight cuts | Anxiety / avoiding social food |
| App logging | 1–4 week education; plateaus | Decimal-place procrastination |
| Hand portions | Default life; travel; maintenance | Drift if palms never recalibrate |

Hand-portion rough map (adult palm/fist — calibrate to *your* hands after a scale week):

| Hand cue | Often maps to |
|---|---|
| Palm of protein | ~20–40 g protein depending on thickness and cut |
| Fist of carbs (cooked) | A meal-sized carb serve for many people |
| Thumb of fats | Oils, nut butters, dressings — easy to under-count |
| Cupped hand of veg | Volume without much calorie drama |

These are teaching tools, not lab assays. After you weigh staples once, your eyes get better.

## A sane sequence

1. **2 weeks** weighed staples (rice, oats, meat, oil, peanut butter) so your eyes calibrate.
2. Switch to **hand portions** + repeat meals — [flexible vs meal plans](/blog/flexible-dieting-vs-meal-plans).
3. Re-weigh for a week if the scale trend dies — [why am I not losing?](/blog/why-am-i-not-losing-fat).
4. Recalculate targets after meaningful weight change — [recalculating calories](/blog/recalculating-calories-after-weight-change).
5. Keep protein targets honest either way — [protein targets](/blog/protein-targets-for-lifters).

On a deep cut, spend more time in scale/app mode. At maintenance or a patient lean bulk, hand portions usually win for sanity.

## What the scale teaches that vibes don’t

- Cooking oils and sauces are silent calorie stacks.
- “A scoop” of peanut butter is rarely one tablespoon.
- Restaurant rice and pasta portions dwarf home serves — hence the separate [restaurant guide](/blog/hitting-macros-at-restaurants).
- Weekend drift is often portion drift, not a mysterious metabolism failure — [weekend warrior damage control](/blog/weekend-warrior-damage-control).

Pair weight with waist and photos when cutting — [tracking weight / waist / photos](/blog/tracking-weight-waist-photos) — so one noisy morning doesn’t rewrite the plan. Sodium and glycogen swing the scale overnight — [sodium / water / scale panic](/blog/sodium-water-scale-panic).

## Common mistakes

- Logging forever with no exit plan into habits
- Never weighing oils once, then blaming “metabolism”
- Hand portions with zero occasional audit after bodyweight changes a lot
- Using the scale as punishment instead of calibration
- Ignoring partner/family cooking constraints — [partner / family food](/blog/partner-family-food-macros)

## Takeaways

Weigh to learn. Portion to live. Audit when stuck. The goal is accurate-enough fuel for training — not a perfect diary — [calories & macros hub](/blog/calories-and-macros-for-lifters) · [Yeh Bro System](/start-here).
