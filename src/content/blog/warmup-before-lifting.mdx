**Scope:** Training quality · before [progressive overload](/blog/progressive-overload) work sets · [Yeh Bro System · training](/start-here#step-2).

A warmup should make the first work set feel **available**, not turn the session into a mobility seminar. Foam rolling your entire posterior chain while the squat rack waits is usually procrastination with a wellness filter.

## TL;DR

- Structure: **raise temperature → prep the patterns you’ll load → ramp to working weight**.
- Ramp sets (empty bar → light → moderate) matter more than twenty minutes of random stretching.
- Longer warmups when you’re older, colder, coming back from time off, or loading heavy hinges/squats.
- Bands are optional tools for openers — not a second workout — [resistance bands](/go/bands-amz).

## The three-layer warmup

### 1. General raise (2–5 minutes)

Walk, easy bike, jump rope — just enough to feel warm and breathe a bit. If you already walked to the gym, you may be done. Context: [walking for lifters](/blog/walking-for-lifters).

### 2. Pattern prep (3–6 minutes)

Do the joint actions you’re about to load:

| Session bias | Useful prep examples |
|---|---|
| Squat day | Bodyweight squats, hip openers, ankle rocks |
| Hinge day | Hip hinge drills, glute bridge, light band pull-throughs |
| Push / pull | Arm circles, band dislocates / face pulls, scap push-ups |
| Overhead | Thoracic openers, light stick presses |

Keep it specific. You’re not “fixing everything” — you’re greasing today’s grooves.

### 3. Ramp sets (the part people skip)

Example for a work weight of ~100 kg squat:

| Set | Load | Reps | Feel |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Empty bar / light | 5–8 | Groove |
| 2 | ~40–50% | 3–5 | Smooth |
| 3 | ~70% | 2–3 | Serious |
| 4 | ~85% | 1–2 | Ready |
| Work | 100% planned | Planned reps @ ~2 RIR | [RPE guide](/blog/rpe-reps-in-reserve) |

Heavier top sets → more ramp patience. Lighter accessories → shorter ramps.

## What warmup is not

| Not required | Why |
|---|---|
| 40-minute foam-roll podcast | Diminishing returns; delays progressive work |
| Max-effort plyometrics before every session | Fatigue cost before the logbook |
| Stretching a cold muscle into the splits | Save long static stretches for after / separate if you care |
| Pre-workout as a warmup substitute | Stim ≠ tissue prep — [caffeine dose](/blog/caffeine-dose-training) |

## Bands without the circus

Light bands help shoulders and hips wake up when cables are busy or you’re in a hotel gym. Use them as **openers**, then move to the barbell or dumbbells.

## Session-type shortcuts

### Full body days

One short general raise, then ramp the **first big lift** thoroughly; later patterns need less ceremony if you’re already warm — [full body template](/blog/beginner-full-body-program).

### Upper / lower

Upper: shoulders + ramp first press/row. Lower: hips/ankles + ramp squat or hinge — [upper / lower](/blog/upper-lower-split).

### Coming back from a break or feeling wrecked

Longer ramps, fewer work sets, swallow the ego. Or take [rest / active recovery](/blog/rest-days-vs-active-recovery). Persistent weird fatigue → [bloodwork literacy](/blog/bloodwork-for-lifters), not a harder warmup.

## Soft close

Warmup is on-ramp, not content. Get warm, prep the pattern, ramp the load, then progress the logbook — [Yeh Bro System](/start-here).
