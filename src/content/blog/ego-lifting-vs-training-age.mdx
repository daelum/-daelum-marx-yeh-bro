Ego lifting is loading the bar for the story you’ll tell — not for the adaptation you’ll keep. **Training age** (how long you’ve been lifting *seriously and consistently*) is the quieter variable that decides whether a weight is “impressive” or just stupid. Confuse the two and you collect injuries, stalled logbooks, and a personality built on one lucky set.

## TL;DR

- **Training age** beats Instagram weight. Year-two lifters shouldn’t chase decade-long numbers.
- Most work sets should leave **1–3 reps in reserve (RIR)** — [RPE guide](/blog/rpe-reps-in-reserve).
- Progress = [progressive overload](/blog/progressive-overload) you can recover from, not a max every Monday.
- Feeds lie with lighting, pumps, and sometimes pharmacology — [natty or not](/blog/natty-or-not-physique-standards). Don’t ego-load to “catch up.”

## What ego lifting looks like in the wild

| Tell | What’s actually happening |
|---|---|
| Half-reps with a crowd | Range of motion sacrificed for plates |
| Spotter bench-presses the set | You didn’t own the weight |
| “PR” after five hours’ sleep and a stim mountain | Debt, not strength — [sleep](/blog/sleep-for-lifters) · [caffeine](/blog/caffeine-dose-training) |
| Form collapses on the last three reps every set | Daily failure cosplay — [coach red flags](/blog/online-coach-red-flags) |
| Program changes every time the bar feels heavy | Avoiding the logbook |

## Training age in plain language

| Rough stage | Honest expectation |
|---|---|
| Months 0–6 | Skill acquisition — [full body](/blog/beginner-full-body-program), leave ego at the door |
| ~6–24 months | Linear-ish progress if sleep/food hold — [upper / lower](/blog/upper-lower-split) when ready |
| Multi-year | Slower PRs, smarter volume, deloads that aren’t optional — [deload](/blog/deload-week) |

Someone with a ten-year training age, great sleep, and maybe undisclosed variables is not your Tuesday competitor. Comparison without context is how people quit — or escalate into grey-market nonsense we don’t source — [PED hub](/blog/ped-harm-reduction-australia).

## Heavy can still be smart

Ego ≠ intensity. Useful hard training:

- Top sets near **1–2 RIR** on main lifts when recovered
- Controlled eccentrics and full ranges you earned
- Testing a rep-max occasionally — not every session
- Belts / chalk when the load justifies them, not for empty-bar curls

Warm up like an adult — [warmup before lifting](/blog/warmup-before-lifting). Read the week honestly — [how to read a training logbook](/blog/training-logbook).

## When the bar feels stuck

Before you add plates out of spite, run the [plateau checklist](/blog/plateau-checklist-for-lifters): sleep, protein, stress, deload, then program tweaks. Creatine still helps training quality for most people who take it daily — [creatine myths](/blog/creatine-myths) · [Muscle Nation creatine monohydrate](/go/creatine-amz) — it does not fix ego mechanics.
