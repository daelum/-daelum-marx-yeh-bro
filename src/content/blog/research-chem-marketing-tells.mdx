“**For research only**,” “not for human consumption,” lab-coat stock photos, purity PDFs from nowhere — these are **marketing tells**, not safety certificates. This page teaches you to recognise the pitch. It does **not** name vendors, doses, or shopping paths. Yeh Bro does not sell or source SARMs, unapproved peptides, or AAS.

## TL;DR

- Research-chem branding is often a **liability costume** for grey retail aimed at gym buyers.  
- Purity claims without accountable manufacturing are vibes — [forum lore vs clinical care](/blog/forum-cycle-lore-vs-clinical-care).  
- Legal risk where you live can be serious; “research” wording is not a force field. Not legal advice.  
- Prefer not escalating; clinicians beat Telegram if someone’s already in deep.

## Classic tells (education)

| Tell | Literacy read |
|---|---|
| “Not for human consumption” on a gym-bro landing page | Marketing / legal theatre — still may be treated as supply risk |
| COA screenshots with no chain of custody | Easy to fake; not your lab |
| Bodybuilding before/after as “research outcomes” | Enhancement sales funnel — [influencer literacy](/blog/enhanced-influencer-media-literacy) |
| Soft language: healing, GH, fat-loss miracles | Claim soup — see [peptide literacy](/blog/peptide-literacy-harm-reduction) |
| Coach upsells vials | [Online coach red flags](/blog/online-coach-red-flags) |
| “Legal tren alternative” | Usually junk or restricted — still not a toy |

## Health & legal unknowns (high-level)

- Unknown dose accuracy / contamination  
- No meaningful long-term safety library for many products in this marketing class  
- Injection / ingestion risk without clinical oversight  
- Customs / controlled-substance exposure depending on jurisdiction (AU: TGA / Border Force context — verify official sources)
