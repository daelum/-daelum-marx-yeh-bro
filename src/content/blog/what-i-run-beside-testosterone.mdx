## Bloodwork and clinician first

If you’re reading this because you train hard and someone mentioned testosterone — start with a real conversation and labs, not a cart.

1. Book care with someone who can interpret **your** history, meds, and goals.
2. Get a **baseline** while you’re stable enough to compare later.
3. Fix sleep, alcohol, deficit aggression, and food pattern before you “buy X because Discord said so.”
4. Only then discuss supplements as **gaps or preferences** — not as medicine cosplay.

Yeh Bro will not diagnose low T from a screenshot or tell you to start therapy. Legal and clinical decisions stay where they belong. Harm-reduction hub when culture talk gets heavier: [PED harm reduction](/blog/ped-harm-reduction-australia).

## Why lipids, BP, liver markers, and vitamins show up

Exogenous testosterone (including prescribed TRT) sits in the same educational conversation as broader androgen culture: markers that clinicians often care about include:

| Watch item | Why it comes up |
|---|---|
| **Lipids** | Androgen patterns and diet can shift HDL / LDL / triglycerides |
| **Blood pressure** | Often paired with labs; home trends only as your clinician advises |
| **Liver enzymes** | Many reasons elevate ALT/AST — alcohol, illness, training, meds, orals in PED talk |
| **Haematocrit / CBC** | Androgens can raise red-cell mass; viscosity talk is clinical |
| **Vitamin D / other micros** | Status varies with sun, food, indoor life — test beats guessing |

None of that means “buy this stack and your panels are handled.” Trends over time, with a clinician, beat one lucky blood draw and a supplement receipt. Deeper PED-only framing: [PED bloodwork literacy](/blog/ped-bloodwork-literacy).
